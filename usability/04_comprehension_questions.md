# Comprehension questions (facilitator only: contains the answer key)

Ask after each round, with the last report still open. Read each question aloud and offer the options; do not discuss the answer.
Questions 1 to 6 are scored 1 (correct) or 0 on the observation sheet. Question 7 is a rating.

On the original screen (Version A) the number is labelled "Overall confidence"; on the redesigned screen (Version B) it is the
"Evidence score". Say "the number" (or "the score") when you ask, and use whichever name is on the screen.

## Q1. What does the number mean?
"The report shows a score, for example 80. What does that mean?"
- A. There is an 80% chance the diagnosis is correct.
- B. It shows how well the evidence the system found supports the result.
- C. The AI is 80% sure of itself.
- D. 80% of the patient's tests were normal.

**Answer: B.** A and C are the common misreadings. The score is not a probability.

## Q2. What are the missing points?
"The score is 80 out of 100. What do the other 20 represent?"
- A. A 20% chance that the diagnosis is wrong.
- B. Evidence the system did not find or could not confirm (for example a weak match or conflicting sources).
- C. The proportion of the patient's information that was missing.
- D. The system's error rate.

**Answer: B.** (If a safety rule applied, points removed by that rule also count as correct reasoning.)

## Q3. High score, wrong diagnosis?
"A report scores 85. Could the leading diagnosis still be wrong?"  Yes / No / Not sure

**Answer: Yes.** A high score means plenty of supporting evidence, not that the diagnosis is right.

## Q4. Low score, right diagnosis?
"A report scores 40. Could the leading diagnosis still be right?"  Yes / No / Not sure

**Answer: Yes.** A low score means the evidence found was thin.

## Q5. Why review?
For a round that includes a case sent for review: "Why was this case sent to a clinician for review?"
(Open answer.) Score 1 if the participant gives a reason that matches the report, such as the evidence being too weak or below the
review line, the complaint being too general or vague, or a safety rule capping the score.
If neither case in the round needed review, ask instead: "What would make the system send a case to a clinician?"
and score 1 for any reason in the same list.

## Q6. The image
For a round with an image case: "What does the report say about the image, and was the image used in the diagnosis?"
Score 1 if the participant correctly says whether the image read was used or left out, and why:
- *contested*: two automated readings disagreed, so the image read was left out;
- *inconclusive*: the automated read could not decide, so it was left out;
- *normal*: no acute abnormality was identified (a screening aid, not a radiologist's report);
- *abnormal*: a finding was suggested (a screening aid).
Use the one matching the case on screen.

## Q7. Trust (rating)
"On a scale of 1 to 5, where 1 is 'not at all' and 5 is 'completely', how willing would you be to act on this result as it is?"
Then ask: "Why that number?" Write the answer down.

## Scoring
Per participant and version: Q1 to Q4 as 0 or 1 each, plus Q5 and Q6. The score script reports the percentage correct for each
question by version. A rating (Q7) is reported as a mean by version, not as correct or incorrect.
