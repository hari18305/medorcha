# Session script (facilitator)

Two people: a **facilitator** who speaks, and a **notetaker** who fills `06_observation_sheet.md` and says nothing.
Total about 35 to 40 minutes. Before the participant arrives: both versions running, one warm-up case done, task cards and forms
printed, the order for this participant read from the table in `README.md`.

## 1. Welcome and consent (4 min)
- Say: "Thank you for helping. We are testing the software, not you. Nothing you say or do can be wrong."
- Hand over the information sheet. Answer questions. Collect the signed consent. Note role and experience.
- Say: "Please think aloud: say what you are looking at, what you expect, and what confuses you. I will not be able to help
  with the screen, because I want to see what it is like when nobody is there to explain it."

## 2. Round 1 (about 13 min)
- Tell the participant which screen they are using (just say "the first version" or "the second version"; do not call one better).
- Give **Task Card 1**, then **Task Card 2** of the assigned case set, one at a time.
- A case takes 30 to 60 seconds to process. Say "it is working, you can wait" if asked.
- **Do not explain anything on the screen.** If they ask "what does this mean?", answer: "What do you think it means?" and note the
  question. If they are stuck for more than 2 minutes, say "let's move on" and mark the task as not completed.
- After the tasks, ask the **comprehension questions** (`04_comprehension_questions.md`, Questions 1 to 6) with the last report still open.
- Hand over the **SUS form** and feedback questions (`05_sus_and_feedback_form.md`). Collect it.

## 3. Short break (1 min)
Offer water. Switch the browser to the other version and open a fresh case form.

## 4. Round 2 (about 13 min)
Same as Round 1 with the other version and the other case set. Use the same comprehension questions and form.

## 5. Debrief (4 min)
Ask, and write the answers down:
1. "Which screen was easier to understand, and why?"
2. "What was the most confusing thing you saw today?"
3. "Was there something you wanted to know that the screen did not tell you?"
4. "Would you use something like this in your work? What would need to be different?"
5. "Any words or numbers you did not understand?"

Thank the participant. Remind them nothing here is advice for patients.

## Rules for the facilitator
- Stay neutral. Never say "good", "correct" or "nice", and never explain a number, a term or a button.
- Do not defend the design, even if the participant criticises it. Say "thank you, that is useful".
- Let silence happen. Ask "what are you thinking?" rather than suggesting an answer.
- If the system errors (for example a provider limit), note it, restart the case once, and record that it was a system fault, not a participant error.
- Do not reveal the comprehension answers until the participant has left.
